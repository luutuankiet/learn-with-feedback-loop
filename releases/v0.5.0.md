# v0.5.0 — Builder-first teaching + one-screen grading

Minor release: two behaviors the learner kept re-requesting mid-session are now encoded in `learn-with-reps`, plus three moves salvaged from Anthropic's official `learn` skill.

## TL;DR

- **First principles — teach the builder, not the consumer.** Foundational concepts now lead with the origin story: the problem that existed first → the design pressures that shaped the solution → the trade-off that remains. Mandatory for design-shaped foundations, proportionate for surface details (a flag's syntax gets a one-liner, not an epic).
- **Grade in one screen.** Every graded rep replays both sides — a one-line recap of the rep + a bullet digest of the learner's answer (typos cleaned, ✓/✗ per claim), with verbatim quotes reserved for when the wording itself is the evidence. Batch repliers never scroll back to reconstruct context.
- **Salvage from Anthropic's `learn` skill:** the impatient-vs-genuinely-stuck distinction in momentum mode (a foothold is not caving), worked-parallel-example as a rep shape, and "a hint that is the answer with extra steps" named explicitly as an answer-leak.

## Why this release exists

Two requests kept recurring inside live sessions:

1. *"Teach me why they're built that way — my identity is a developer; I get satisfaction from learning to build things, not mindlessly consuming."* The dense-turn table already asked for "the design reason"; that slot is now a full discipline with an origin-story structure and a proportionality rule.
2. *"Quote my answer back — I reply in batches and don't remember my exact words when I read your grading a turn later."* With 3–5 reps per turn, grading that doesn't replay the rep and the answer forces a scroll-back every time.

Both were being re-requested per session — the definition of behavior that belongs in the skill, not in chat.

Separately, Anthropic shipped an official `learn` skill. Most of it conflicts with this repo's dense-multi-topic design (their "keep turns short" doctrine is the opposite bet), but three moves were genuinely additive and are folded in. The dense-turn philosophy is unchanged.
